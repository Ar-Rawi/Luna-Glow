## 1. Solunar Theory & Lunar Mechanics

### 1.1 The Solunar Principle (John Alden Knight Engine)
The **Solunar Theory** states that the biological feeding rhythms of freshwater and saltwater fish are driven by the gravitational position of the Moon relative to the Earth.

- **Major Feeding Periods (Duration: 2 Hours)**
  - **Moon Overhead (Lunar Transit):** Occurs when the moon is at its highest point in the sky directly above your position. Fish activity reaches daily absolute peaks.
  - **Moon Underfoot (Lunar Antipode):** Occurs 12.4 hours after transit when the moon is directly below on the opposite side of the planet. Second strongest feeding window.
- **Minor Feeding Periods (Duration: 1 Hour)**
  - **Moonrise:** As the moon breaks the horizon, fish undergo a short burst of aggressive activity.
  - **Moonset:** As the moon dips below the horizon, secondary ambush predators strike.

### 1.2 Spring Tides vs. Neap Tides
- **Spring Tides (Full Moon & New Moon)**
  - **Mechanics:** The Sun, Earth, and Moon align in a straight line (Syzygy). Combined gravitational forces create the maximum water displacement.
  - **Angler Advantage:** Strong current velocities flush baitfish, crabs, and shrimp out of mangroves, estuaries, and mudflats. **PEAK FEEDING DAYS (Solunar Index: 90–100/100)**.
- **Neap Tides (First Quarter & Third Quarter)**
  - **Mechanics:** Sun and Moon sit at 90° right angles. Minimal gravitational pull.
  - **Angler Reality:** Water motion is sluggish ("Slack Water"). Fish become inactive and hide near deep structures.

## 2. Tidal Dynamics & Water Movement Patterns

### 2.1 Reading the Tide Windows
1. **Incoming / Flood Tide (1 to 2 Hours Before High Tide):**
   - **Best Overall Window!** Cool, oxygenated oceanic water rushes into estuaries and bays. Predators push into shallow flats and river mouths to feed.
2. **High Slack Water:**
   - Brief 30-minute window when water stops moving at peak high tide. Activity drops. Target deep holes.
3. **Outgoing / Ebb Tide (First 2 Hours After High Tide):**
   - Water drains out of mangroves. Predators stage at channel drop-offs, creek mouths, and point structures to ambush fleeing bait.
4. **Low Slack Water:**
   - Lowest water level. Target deep drop-offs, ocean passes, and structure where fish pool together.

## 3. Target Species Profiles & Tactical Guide

### 3.1 Saltwater & Coastal Predators

#### 1. Barramundi / Asian Seabass (*Lates calcarifer*)
- **Optimal Moon Phase:** 2 days before to 2 days after **New Moon** and **Full Moon**.
- **Preferred Tide:** First 2 hours of Outgoing Tide (draining mangroves) or incoming flood tide at creek mouths.
- **Top Baits & Lures:** Live mullet, live prawns, 100mm-120mm deep diving minnows, soft plastic paddle tails (paddle slow near structure).
- **Strategy:** Cast close to snags, fallen trees, and rock bars. Allow lure to pause near structure.

#### 2. Snapper & Mangrove Jack (*Lutjanus species*)
- **Optimal Moon Phase:** Full Moon nights and New Moon dark nights.
- **Preferred Tide:** Strong Spring Tides during low-light dawn/dusk hours.
- **Top Baits & Lures:** Live prawns, fresh squid strips, weedless soft plastics, vibration jigs.
- **Strategy:** Fish extremely close to jetty pilings, pontoon structures, and coral reefs. Use heavy fluorocarbon leaders (30lb–50lb) to prevent bust-offs.

#### 3. Giant Trevally (GT) & Pelagics (*Caranx ignobilis*)
- **Optimal Moon Phase:** Full Moon & New Moon (Spring Tides).
- **Preferred Tide:** High velocity current seams at island points, ocean passes, and headlands.
- **Top Baits & Lures:** 160mm–200mm Topwater Poppers, Stickbaits, Metal Jigs.
- **Strategy:** Aggressive fast popping on surface current rips during Solunar Major Overhead periods.

#### 4. Grouper & Coral Trout (*Epinephelinae*)
- **Optimal Moon Phase:** First Quarter to Full Moon transition.
- **Preferred Tide:** Turn of the tide (Slack to Flood) around deep drop-offs and bommies.
- **Top Baits & Lures:** Live crab, octopus tentacles, heavy slow-pitch jigs.
- **Strategy:** Lock down drag completely. Strike instantly to pull fish out of bottom caves.

## 4. Bait Selection & Rigging Masterclass

### 4.1 Live Bait vs. Artificial Lures
- **Live Prawns / Shrimp:** The universal bait. Hook through the tail segment or carapace avoiding the black brain spot.
- **Live Mullet / Sardines:** Hook through the nose nostrils for trolling/retrieving or back dorsal fin for stationary float fishing.
- **Topwater Poppers:** Best used in calm early morning conditions or during Solunar Minor Moonrise periods over shallow reefs.
- **Soft Plastics:** Rig on a weedless jighead to bounce along bottom contours where predators ambush prey.

### 4.2 Essential Rigs
1. **Carolina / Running Sinker Rig:**
   - Main Braid $\rightarrow$ Swivel $\rightarrow$ Sinker $\rightarrow$ Leader (30–80cm) $\rightarrow$ Circle Hook. Allows bait to move naturally in current.
2. **Paternoster / Dropper Rig:**
   - Heavy sinker at bottom with 2 dropper loops above. Ideal for deep sea bottom fishing over reefs.

## 5. Master Knots Guide

1. **FG Knot (100% Strength):** Connecting Main Braid to Fluorocarbon/Mono Leader. Essential for heavy GT & Snapper fishing.
2. **Palomar Knot:** Connecting Lures/Hooks to Leader. Unbeatable 95%+ knot strength.
3. **Loop Knot (Non-Slip Loop):** Attaching lures to allow 360° natural swimming action.

## 6. Weather & Barometric Pressure Factors

- **Rising / High Barometer (1015+ hPa):** Clear skies, calm water, steady fish feeding.
- **Rapidly Falling Barometer (Pre-Frontal):** **THE ULTIMATE FEEDING FRENZY!** Fish sense approaching storm pressure drops and feed voraciously before the front hits.
- **Post-Frontal Low Barometer:** Water cools, fish seek deep shelter. Slow down retrieve speeds.
